ScouTirol — foreground service

Demonstration of FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION for the Google Play declaration. Package com.scoutirol, version 2.3.

Why the app needs the permission

ScouTirol plans and records hikes, bike rides and drives in South Tyrol. A recording has to survive a locked screen: people put the phone in a pocket and walk for hours. Without a foreground service Android stops delivering location updates, and the recorded track loses exactly the part that was walked in the meantime.

What the video shows

One real recording, filmed on 29 September 2026 on a Galaxy S23 running Android 16. It has a single cut, marked by a title card, because the phone was away from the recording computer while its owner was walking.

  1. Before the tap. The “Record” tab, 0.0 km, and the button “Aufzeichnung starten” (“start recording”). Nothing is running.
  2. The user taps it. The button becomes “Stoppen & speichern” (“stop & save”), the timer starts, and a real GPS fix places the blue dot on the map. The hint below the numbers reads: “Aufzeichnung läuft – du kannst das Handy einstecken und sperren. In der Leiste steht so lange eine Meldung.” (“Recording is running – you can pocket and lock the phone. A notification stays in the status bar while it does.”)
  3. The notification. The drawer shows the ongoing notification “ScouTirol zeichnet auf — Die Aufzeichnung läuft weiter, auch wenn der Bildschirm aus ist” (“ScouTirol is recording — the recording keeps running even when the display is off”).
  4. The screen goes off and the phone locks.
  5. The title card covers the walk: 64 minutes with the screen off and locked, the phone in a pocket, 4.6 km on foot.
  6. After the walk. The same recording is still running: 4.6 km, 64:13, and the walked route drawn on the map. The user taps “Stoppen & speichern”, names the tour and saves it.

What was measured while the screen was off

The phone was reconnected before it was unlocked, so the device state below was read over ADB while the display was still off. These are the numbers behind the video.

About the cut and the black areas

The recording computer cannot follow the phone on a walk, so the screen capture was stopped before the walk and started again afterwards. The title card marks that gap and states what happened during it. The app’s own recording ran without interruption across the cut — that is what the point count and the distance above show.

Two things are masked in black, both belonging to the device owner rather than to the app: the status bar throughout (carrier, clock, status icons), and, in the drawer section, the three unrelated notifications below the ScouTirol one. Nothing that ScouTirol displays was removed, blurred or altered.

Scope

  • Recording starts only on an explicit tap and ends when the user ends it.
  • The positions stay on the device. There is no server and no account.
  • The app does not request ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION. The service is always started from the visible app.
  • The clip was recorded with a development build of the same version 2.3; the bundle under review is the release build of that version.
  • Privacy policy: dilidjent.github.io/scoutirol-privacy/

Kurz auf Deutsch

Die App zeichnet auf Wunsch eine Tour auf. Damit die Spur kein Loch bekommt, sobald der Bildschirm zugeht, läuft die Ortung in einem Vordergrunddienst vom Typ location weiter. Die Aufzeichnung beginnt ausschließlich auf Knopfdruck und endet, sobald der Nutzer sie beendet. Solange sie läuft, steht die Meldung „ScouTirol zeichnet auf“ in der Leiste. Die Positionen bleiben auf dem Gerät.

Der Film hat einen einzigen Schnitt, und die Zwischentafel sagt, was in ihm passiert ist: 64 Minuten mit ausgeschaltetem, gesperrtem Bildschirm, das Handy in der Tasche, 4,6 km zu Fuß. Die Aufzeichnung der App lief über den Schnitt hinweg durch — aus einem Punkt wurden 554. Die schwarzen Flächen decken Privates des Gerätebesitzers ab: durchgehend die Statusleiste, im Leisten-Abschnitt die drei fremden Meldungen unter der von ScouTirol. An der Anzeige der App wurde nichts verändert.
